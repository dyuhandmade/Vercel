1. Executive Summary
DYU Handmade will be rebuilt as a premium, story-first digital commerce experience that combines botanical editorial storytelling, handcrafted product discovery and a highly polished Shopify-powered buying flow. The website should feel like entering a quiet botanical atelier rather than entering a conventional online store. Commerce remains essential, but the primary persuasion model is trust and emotional connection: visitors first understand the product, ingredients, craft and ritual; only then does the interface progressively strengthen the purchase action.
The experience direction comes from the supplied Digital Atelier concept, the DYU brand references supplied by the client, the Kadam Haat ambience benchmark, and the eight UI/UX concept images created for this project. The website must preserve DYU's soft natural tone while advancing beyond template commerce through purposeful animation, tactile editorial composition, selective 3D product/ingredient experiences and a strong mobile journey.
Shopify will be integrated as the operational commerce system so the DYU team can manage products, variants, prices, inventory, collections, media, discounts and orders without developer involvement. The custom frontend will transform that commerce data into the brand experience.

5. Design and Brand Experience System

Figure 3. Working palette derived from the botanical atelier direction; final tokens require brand sign-off.
5.1 Typography and layout
Editorial serif headings with grounded, book-like rhythm; highly readable sans-serif for utility and commerce UI.
Generous margins and negative space; avoid crowded card grids and compressed text blocks.
12-column desktop grid with responsive reductions for tablet/mobile.
Use torn-paper, botanical illustration and handwritten-note motifs selectively - not as a decorative layer on every section.
Photography must contextualize products with ingredients, hands, materials, environments and ritual rather than relying only on isolated packshots.
5.2 Tone of voice
Calm, honest and knowledgeable.
Educate before selling.
Sensory language may describe texture, aroma and ritual when it is factually approved.
Avoid false scarcity, exaggerated efficacy language and generic luxury clichés.

7. Experience Funnel and User Journeys

Figure 4. Required narrative sequence for product discovery.
7.1 Primary product journey
Arrive through home, collection, search, social or organic landing page.
See the product in context with a strong emotional/visual opening.
Understand the origin story and what need/ritual the product was created around.
Explore how the product is made and what materials/ingredients contribute to it.
See how the product is used and how the ritual feels.
Read real customer/maker proof and reassurance.
Reach the purchase module with price, variant, quantity, optional gift/subscription information and Add to Bag.
Review cart and continue into Shopify checkout.
7.2 Returning-user shortcut
The story must never become a barrier. Returning users need persistent navigation, search and a discoverable add-to-bag path. On mobile, a compact sticky commerce control may appear after the core product identity is established, while the page continues to encourage story exploration.

13. Motion, 3D and Interaction Requirements

Figure 7. Motion system: progressive intensity with accessibility fallback.
13.1 Signature animation opportunities
Hero product depth: subtle pointer parallax / camera shift, not constant rotation.
Scroll-driven transition from raw botanical to finished product.
Ingredient Atlas: botanical nodes or ingredient objects reveal details on hover/tap.
Craft timeline: sections reveal in sequence as the visitor moves through the process.
Image masks and line-by-line type reveals for editorial pacing.
Product texture or container 360/3D interaction only where real high-quality source assets are supplied.
13.2 Motion guardrails
No auto-playing motion that blocks content access.
No animation required to understand essential product information.
Respect prefers-reduced-motion with static/shortened alternatives.
Avoid simultaneous parallax layers that compromise legibility or motion comfort.
Lazy-load heavyweight animation/3D bundles.
Mobile may simplify or replace desktop 3D scenes with optimized alternatives.
14. Responsive and Mobile UX
The mobile experience is not a compressed desktop design. It should preserve the same emotional sequence while converting wide editorial compositions into swipeable, stacked and tap-friendly modules.

A complete mobile journey concept is included in the Concept Image Gallery. The production implementation must be validated on real devices, including mid-range Android hardware commonly used in India.
15. Accessibility, SEO and Performance
15.1 Accessibility requirements
Semantic heading order and landmark regions.
Keyboard access to navigation, menus, product variants, gallery, ingredient interactions and cart controls.
Visible focus states that fit the brand system.
Alt text for meaningful imagery and decorative treatment for purely ornamental graphics.
Sufficient contrast for text and controls; do not place essential copy over uncontrolled photography without a contrast treatment.
Screen-reader labels for icon-only controls.
Reduced-motion support.
Form labels, error messaging and status announcements for cart interactions.
15.2 SEO requirements
Indexable server-rendered product, collection, story and journal content.
Unique title/meta description and canonical URL strategy.
Product, BreadcrumbList, Organization and Article structured data where eligible and factually supported.
XML sitemap and robots configuration.
Clean handles and internal linking between products, ingredients, collections and articles.
Open Graph / social preview metadata.
